# Review — #1262: the daemon transmits without keying on five paths

Reviewer: Fable (adversarial). Date: 2026-09-04. Reviewed **before implementation**. Verdict: *implement the helper, with four corrections* — two of my premises were false, my proposed alternative was disqualified by an existing test, and the review found a failure mode the fix itself creates.

## Prompt

Carried the defect (five unguarded `engine.transmit*` sites in `lib.rs` against five guards all living in `server.rs`), the observation that three comments already justify the §97.119 callsign gate by saying these paths "key the transmitter" when they do not, and the #1250 precedent. Seven questions asked as falsification attempts: threading versus a higher seam; double-keying; whether a guard may cross an `.await`; whether the handshake is a transmit-then-listen shape; whether the relay should be keyed at all given #1253; the gate's positive control; and scope.

## The alternative I proposed was disqualified — by an existing test

Keying once around `process_received_bytes` instead of at five sites fails on three independent grounds:

1. **It would hold PTT across the QSY scan.** `execute_qsy_actions` contains `tokio::time::sleep(scan_dwell_ms).await` and `engine.receive(..)` per candidate frequency, *between* its two emission sites. Keyed, that is N × dwell of dead carrier, and the SNR the scan reads would be what a transmitting rig hears — nothing. `scan_complete` would then pick a channel on garbage.
2. **Most received bursts emit nothing** — an incomplete SAR fragment, a CONREQ that fails verification or is addressed elsewhere, a QSY line the callsign gate refuses. Keying on arrival keys for every frame heard. That is the #1178 shape, and `twin_daemon_bridge.rs:579` `an_ota_receiver_does_not_key_ptt_at_a_peers_uncoded_traffic` asserts **exactly zero** `PttChanged{true}`. **The alternative fails an existing gate on arrival.**
3. **It inverts the §97.119 gate**, which exists to refuse keying *before* the callsign check.

## Two of my premises were false

**`PttKeyGuard` is `Send`.** I asked whether holding it across an `.await` is sound; it would compile even under a `Send` bound. The right rule is structural rather than type-level — *no guard crosses an await* — and a synchronous helper makes that unwriteable rather than merely documented. Both async sites already complete their `.await`s (mode locks) before the emission.

**#1253 does not implicate the daemon.** I asked whether keying an unauthenticated relay forward makes things worse. #1253's own scope line says `openpulse-mesh` only; the daemon's `maybe_relay_forward` goes through `RelayForwarder::forward`, which verifies the Ed25519 origin signature with `require_authentication` on by default (audit E3) and applies the operator's allow/deny list, consulting no peer-cache trust byte. Relay is also off by default. So there is no authentication reason to withhold keying — and **not keying is worse**: the forward already reaches the sound card, so on a VOX rig it goes on air outside the watchdog with no `PttChanged` edge.

## A trap the review caught before I hit it

Do **not** put `tokio::task::block_in_place` inside the helper to match the `server.rs` sites. `lib.rs` has 49 `#[tokio::test]` functions on the default current-thread flavor, where `block_in_place` panics. That the `lib.rs` sites block a runtime worker is a real but separate defect — filed as #1264.

## A failure mode the fix itself creates

`transmit_handshake_frame` returned `()`, and the CONACK responder falls through to `record_verified_peer`. The F5 note at that call site says explicitly that a peer which never received a CONACK must not be recorded — and a refused PTT assert is that situation, arrived at a third way. The helper therefore returns whether anything went out, and the responder skips the record on failure.

## Scope changes adopted

**Migrate the five existing `server.rs` guards onto the same helper.** A scan cannot verify the scoping of an inline guard, so leaving them inline would mean shipping either an allowlist or a scan with a hole. Ten sites, one shape, one scan.

This forced a design refinement the review did not specify: four of those five sites distinguish an *assert* failure from a *transmit* failure with different messages and different log levels — the station ID logs at `error` because a refused assert is a §97.119 obligation unmet. A plain `Option` return would have flattened that and deleted diagnostics added by the 2026-07-19 audit, so the helper returns `KeyedTxError::{Assert, Transmit}` and every site keeps its exact message.

**Scan every `src/*.rs`**, not just the two files with known defects — #1262 exists precisely because the previous fix was applied per file.

## Gate requirements, including one the review anticipated and one it did not

There is **no natural positive control** here: in #1250 the station-ID path keyed before the fix, so the spy seeing it proved the spy was wired. No `lib.rs` site keyed before this change. Each test therefore keys the shared PTT *directly* first and asserts the spy counted it, ruling out the same vacuity by a different route.

The production-entry test caught a vacuity of its own during implementation. As first written it **passed against the unfixed daemon**, because `cfg_auto` leaves auto-ID on and the station-ID path did key — so the assertion was seeing the periodic ID, not the handshake. The timing exposed it: 10.5 s pre-fix versus 0.47 s post-fix. With auto-ID disabled the handshake is the only possible source of a key, and the test fails pre-fix with "A transmitted a CONREQ without keying the transmitter". Its sibling asserting *zero* keying at a peer's uncoded traffic brackets the property from the other side, which is what rules out the disqualified alternative.

## Split out rather than folded in

**#1263** — `SharedPtt` has no nesting semantics: a second key adopts the first's deadline and the first release drops a transmitter someone else still holds. Pre-existing, cross-crate, and it needs a decision (refuse / nest / adopt) rather than a patch. **#1264** — the `block_in_place` gap above. **#1265** — `ConnectPeer` announces `RfConnectionChanged{connected:true}` before the CONREQ is transmitted; #1199 closed announce-before-*build*, and a refused PTT makes announce-before-*transmit* observable for the first time.
